Why Thinking Matters in Additional Mathematics
Additional Mathematics asks for more than a formula you remember. Each exercise asks you to read a question closely, choose a method, work through it step by step and check that the answer makes sense, often against a domain, a graph or the original equation.
Good questions do not ask “What is the answer?” They ask “How did you work it out?” Examiners reward the process of thinking and clear working, not just the final line. In this guide, you build four big thinking habits:
Break it down
SMALLER STEPS
Split a long question into parts and cases. Solve one at a time.
Spot the pattern
FUNCTION TYPE
Recognise the kind of function: line, quadratic, modulus, fraction.
Picture it
SKETCH THE GRAPH
Sketch the graph to see the range, crossings and turning points.
Check each step
DOMAIN AND METHOD
Follow a clear method. Check each answer against the domain.
